# 04 — The makers, and everything around the films

How the people who made the Iron Man films (and the wikis, comics, spin-offs, and real-world imitators) thought about Tony Stark and his AIs. Researched 2026-11-02.

**Quote markers**
- **[V]** verbatim, read by me in the source text linked (or a full-text republication of it).
- **[V2]** verbatim but read only as quoted in secondary coverage (original not reachable: GQ video, Facebook note, Collider, etc.).
- **[P]** paraphrase / attribution I could not confirm word for word.

## 1. The HUD and interface designers

### 1a. Iron Man (2008): The Orphanage, Pixel Liberation Front, Favreau. The best single source.
**"Heads up: the oral history of 'Iron Man's' original HUD"**, Ian Failes, befores & afters / vfxblog, 2019. https://beforesandafters.com/2019/03/18/heads-up-the-oral-history-of-iron-mans-original-hud
Voices: John Nelson (VFX supervisor), Kent Seki (Pixel Liberation Front, previs/HUD design), Dav Rauch (HUD design supervisor, The Orphanage), Jonathan Rothbart (VFX supervisor, The Orphanage), Wesley Sewell (VFX producer), Kyle McCulloch (comp).

What it says:
- **The HUD's job was to stage a conversation between Tony and JARVIS.** Nelson [V]: *"We were trying to get an organic conversation going between his A.I. assistant J.A.R.V.I.S. [Paul Bettany] and Tony Stark [Downey Jr.]. But no one really knew what that meant."*
- **Favreau's answer to "who's driving, Tony or JARVIS?"** Rauch recounting Favreau [V]: *"They're having a conversation and they're in the dialogue, and sometimes RDJ is asking a question to J.A.R.V.I.S. and other times J.A.R.V.I.S. is asking a question of RDJ. Or one is saying something to the other, or sometimes the other is saying something to the other."* And: *"Look into his eyes. If you look into his eyes you will know. Is he being asked a question or is he asking the question?"* So the initiative is mixed, and the human's attention shows who has it.
- **Three ways information reaches Tony.** Seki [V]: *"There were three different ways he could get this information. First, Jarvis could present it to him both visually and verbally. Or, he could verbally ask for it. And thirdly, he could use his eye movement to bring or activate aspects of the HUD forward. This was a technology called 'artificial foveation'. … The single most important thing about each element in the HUD was that they had a specific purpose. The audience would never know all of this backstory, but they would 'feel' them as long as we stayed true to form following function."*
- **Purpose or cut.** Rauch [V]: *"If it doesn't do something that would be real for somebody flying around in this suit then we are taking it out." Because we figured that Tony is an engineer, and he's going to put stuff in there that works. … everything has to have a purpose and everything has to work and if it doesn't work then it's out."* That rule is what turned the work around after months of placeholder HUDs.
- **John Nelson's three rules (relayed by Seki) [V]:** (1) *"They needed to be driven by Robert's performance. … You could obscure the performance with the graphics sometimes, but at the same time you had to let the performance drive where the graphics go."* (2) Use Z-space: *"The implied Z-space depth behind the camera was just as important as what the audience could see."* (3) *"You should have an alpha event happen in each of the shots … a story point that you hit home with a graphic. For example … Robert would say (in regards to his power level), 'Leave it up on the screen.' Then, instead of appearing and disappearing, the power meter stayed up on the screen. Most alpha events weren't noticeable or even obvious; however, they reinforced the subtext of the moment."* The user can pin something, and the system respects it.
- **The suit as an extension of Tony, and it evolves with him.** Seki [V]: *"John Nelson always saw the suit and its accompanying technology as an extension of Tony. In this way, Tony's technological evolution reflected his own evolution as a scientist, inventor, and ultimately as a character."*
- **Busy at first, then consolidated for an expert.** Rauch [V]: *"we actually intentionally made that first one, the Mark II, really kind of super busy, with lots of detail, lots of numbers … Our idea was that he'll evolve, he'll get more sophisticated, he'll realise once he's flying around that he doesn't want all that."* For the Mark III, mode switching was dropped for *"one mode that would allow him to access each mode as an expert user"*, and the dock collapsed into an "Omega widget" holding *"two to four things that he would want all the time … flight … his health"* [V].
- **Fly in, fly out, not a fixed fighter HUD.** Rothbart [V]: *"fighter jets have HUDs, but they're pretty much static … We wanted it to be moving, but we also didn't want to make cluttered. Which is why we kind of came up with this concept of things flying in and flying out, so that we didn't overload all this data in front of his face while he was talking and acting."*
- **The eye cursor was cut because intent was legible without it.** Rauch [V]: *"The hypothesis was that we'd need an 'eye cursor' to know that he was looking at things … But then when watching it, Favreau realised that it was obvious what was going on … the audience didn't need an eye cursor."* Rothbart [V]: *"it just follows his eyes and figures out what he wants based on where he's looking."*
- **Favreau's brief was the iPhone.** Seki [V]: *"He pulled out his iPhone, which was a new thing at the time"* — *"I want it to feel intuitive like my iPhone."* Rauch [V]: *"You're not designing a prop … You are designing a character."*
- **Tony's UI against Stane's.** Seki [V]: *"Obadiah probably stole Tony's underlying tech for the HUD, maybe the operating system, but not the UI that ran it. … Tony really was the master inventor who would, of course, create an elegant UI."* Same machine, different relationship.
- **An explanatory scene was cut.** A storyboarded and shot sequence explaining the mask sensors was removed after test audiences said *"'Oh duh, it's a heads up display'"* [V]. Users understood the "thing that tells me all the things that I want to know" without a tutorial.
- **The HUD keeps the human visible.** Nelson [V]: *"What the HUD really helped with is bringing everyone back to showing that there was a human in there."*

**Andy Polaine, "Iron Man's HUD and interaction design"** (30 May 2008), quoting Rauch's interview in *Desktop* magazine. https://www.polaine.com/2008/05/iron-mans-hud-and-interaction-design/
- Favreau via Rauch, the 2008 version of the same quote [V2]: *"He's having a conversation with Jarvis, it depends on who's asking the question."*
- Rauch [V2]: *"Amber is kind of the 80s and cyan is the 90s, what's the colour of the future going to look like?"* and design *"starts out complex and then gets more simplified."*
- Polaine's own critique: these are real interaction problems "only a tiny bit in the future", and he names Clippy as the failure case for an assistant that talks to you.

### 1b. Iron Man 2 (2010): Prologue, Perception, and the holographic workshop
- **Prologue Films** (Kyle Cooper, VFX supervisor/producer; Danny Yount, designer) did the HUD and holograms. Motionographer (Justin Cone), not the designers [V]: *"Prologue lifted screen design elements off of flat surfaces and into the three dimensional world surrounding Tony Stark"*, and *"Prologue's challenge was to choreograph Downey Jr.'s finger-snapping, wrist-flicking bravura with an incredibly intricate graphical system."* The graphics give Stark his "eureka" moment (the new element). https://motionographer.com/features/prologue-iron-man-2 ; Gizmodo's write-up: https://gizmodo.com/5578966/the-making-of-iron-man-2s-incredible-interfaces (no designer quotes).
- **Perception** (John LePore, Jeremy Lasky, Danny Gonzalez) did 125+ shots, including the household screens, the glass coffee table, and Tony's glass phone. Engadget, 2015: https://www.engadget.com/2015-12-14-perception-ui-design.html . Perception made the glass phone by cutting a slab of glass to a phone shape and shooting a demo video, which won them the gadget work [P, Engadget summary]. Their stated brief was "the visual manifestation of J.A.R.V.I.S. itself in a fully immersive holographic environment" [P, from the Perception project page via search snippet; the page itself would not load]. LePore's best-known line, *"the UI itself is the personality"* [V2], is about Arnim Zola's computer in *Winter Soldier*, not JARVIS. Engadget says Samsung later borrowed Perception ideas for TouchWiz.
- **John Underkoffler** (Oblong; *Minority Report*'s science advisor) was also the science advisor on *Iron Man*. MIT News [V]: he *"served as scientific advisor for both films"*, which *"both depicted similar technologies"*, and film work is *"the process of refining and simplifying those ideas so they're instantly legible on screen."* https://news.mit.edu/2014/startup-oblong-industries-gesture-control-interface-0905 . I found no source tying g-speak directly to the workshop holograms. Rauch names *Minority Report* as his inspiration in the oral history.

### 1c. The Avengers (2012) and Iron Man 3 (2013): Jayse Hansen and Cantina Creative
- **ProVideo Coalition, "Marvel's The Avengers: Taking the HUD to new heights"**: https://www.provideocoalition.com/marvels_the_avengers_taking_the_hud_to_new_heights/
  - Hansen [V]: *"The HUD was a character, Tony's computer named Jarvis, and each shot had a specific message to deliver."*
  - Hansen [V]: *"Everything in the HUD, even down to the tiny micro-text, relates precisely to the current story-point."*
  - Whedon asked for *"several modes – Diagnostic, Battle, and Flight"*, plus a *"2D radar-map that could unfold into a 3D representation of his environment, trajectories and potential threats"* [V]. Lawes (Cantina) [V]: *"Joss had the idea of making it spin from one mode to another … two HUDs in one: A standard mode and a battle mode."*
  - Hansen consulted an A-10 pilot [V]: *"I asked him how he would want his 'Ultimate HUD' to work in various situations."* The actors also improvised with the screens on set, so content was designed to match what they did.
- **Pushing Pixels, "conversation with Jayse Hansen"** (2012): https://www.pushing-pixels.org/?p=7447
  - [V] *"This guy's got so much info coming at him from Jarvis, his suit computer. How does he read it?"*
  - [V] The diagnostic is about *"how to display it in a way that is understandable and useful to the user at a glance."*
  - [V] The pilot said *"the first thing he teaches his students is how to focus on the important stuff as you need it"*.
  - [V] *"we made sure that all the text is related to the scene, and all the graphics had a purpose"*. And candidly: *"I'm not worried about how a programmer is going to have to make this all work."*
- **fxguide, "Iron Man 3: more suits to play with"**: https://www.fxguide.com/featured/iron-man-3-more-suits-to-play-with/
  - Hristova (Cantina) [V]: the HUD's laser lights are *"a motif of where the actual holograms emanate from"*, with a shelf that uses Z-depth and graphics that *"feel like glass holograms that could exist in a photoreal sense."*
  - Lawes [V]: *"We try and bounce off the suit design and incorporate design motifs from the suit into the HUD."* Each IM3 suit (Shotgun, Nightclub, Hammerhead) got a HUD styled to its purpose. Marvel's Chris Townsend pushed toward 3D photoreal holograms [P, Maxon press].
  - Chinese Theatre reconstruction, Bath (VFX) [V]: *"especially when Stark was moving his hands to control Jarvis … We built all of Stark's garage in 3D because we needed to be able to have our holograms intersect and collide with those objects."* The holograms live in his real room.
  - Trixter on the empty suit massaging Pepper's shoulders [V]: *"We had to make it seem like RDJ was in the suit even though he wasn't."*
- Hansen's Iron Man 3 process reel (Cantina, John Likens): https://vimeo.com/66944464 . The Avengers HUD breakdown: https://vimeo.com/44134435

## 2. Writers, directors, actors

- **Jon Favreau (Iron Man, 2008), on why JARVIS is not a butler.** CBR, "Director Jon Favreau talks 'Iron Man'" [V]: *"In the case of Jarvis, we decided to not have it be an Alfred-type butler but take a leap there."* https://www.cbr.com/director-jon-favreau-talks-iron-man/ . This is the citation the MCU wiki uses for "to avoid any confusion between Jarvis and Batman's butler, Alfred Pennyworth". Wikipedia also says JARVIS was "modeled after H.O.M.E.R." (a comics AI of Stark's), uncited. In the same interview Favreau calls Tony's early attitude to weapons [V] *"a game to him, it's a big video game, and it looks like one."*
- **Favreau's casting brief, via Bettany** (GQ "Breaks Down His Most Iconic Characters" video, quoted by We Got This Covered) [V2]: *"I got a phone call from Jon Favreau saying 'I need the voice of a sort of personality-less robot and I thought of you immediately.' [Laughs] I thought it was the funniest thing I'd ever heard so I said yes."* https://wegotthiscovered.com/movies/paul-bettany-jarvis-vision-mcu-casting/ ; video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nGIwJ53XpxU (local transcription failed, see §5). The dryness comes from casting a dry actor to play "personality-less", not from writing jokes for him.
- **Paul Bettany on the recording.** SuperHeroHype, May 2008 (via Wikipedia; the site returned 403 to me) [V2]: *"I didn't know I was in a superhero movie … I really didn't know what I was going in for because it was two hours in a recording booth."* He did it as a favor to Favreau, whom he knew from *Wimbledon*. https://www.superherohype.com/?p=96605 . I found no confirmed source on whether Downey and Bettany recorded together. Several low-quality pages say Bettany recorded alone in a booth, which fits the "two hours" account but is not confirmed [P].
- **Downey and the absent voice.** No direct quote found. The closest primary account is the oral history above. Nelson [V]: *"We used a beam splitter on the front of the camera so Robert could look at it and react … if you get something in front of Robert to react to, he's got 'world-class' reaction. It's like he's in the moment."* Rothbart [V]: *"we would call out directions and have Robert look at those areas."* In other words, Downey played JARVIS against called-out cues and a lens, and the graphics were built afterwards to fit his eyes. In 2016 Downey offered to voice Zuckerberg's Jarvis as a joke, if Zuckerberg paid Bettany (to a charity) [P, BGR/ScreenCrush].
- **Joss Whedon on Vision (Age of Ultron).** [V2, IGN via Flickering Myth]: *"I wanted Paul to play this part since before I wanted to make Avengers movies."* He would not say pre-release how JARVIS related to Vision. He called Ultron "our new Frankenstein myth" and said Ultron "needs to be the brainchild of the Avengers", that is, Tony's [V2, Empire via MovieWeb / East Idaho News]. https://www.flickeringmyth.com/2015/03/joss-whedon-discusses-the-vision-and-ultron-from-avengers-age-of-ultron/
- **Kevin Feige on Iron Man 3's theme** (no direct Shane Black quote found) [V2]: *"the love triangle in this movie is between Tony, Pepper and his obsession with those suits, and the obsession with technology."* Black framed IM3 as a "technological thriller" with a "Christmas Carol aspect". The film's argument, that Tony is Iron Man and the suits are a crutch for his anxiety, is the reading of critics and Feige. I did not confirm a Black statement that says it directly.
- **Kerry Condon (FRIDAY, 2015–2019).** Secondary only: in a Collider interview reported by CinemaBlend, she says she was told to deliver *"Life functions critical"* in Endgame "as sad as possible", and that secrecy meant she didn't know the full scene [V2/P]. https://www.cinemablend.com/news/2559527/marvels-friday-actress-reveals-heartbreaking-response-to-iron-mans-death ; https://theplaylist.net/kerry-condon-talks-keeping-iron-mans-endgame-death-under-wraps-marvel-secrets-will-drive-you-to-drink-20240429/ . Her own accent was kept: the Irish-slang line *"Targeting system's knackered, boss!"* (Civil War) was a hit in Ireland (MCU wiki, citing the Daily Edge). The British JARVIS gave way to an Irish FRIDAY with no official reason given that I could find. Bettany's and Condon's own voices simply stayed.
- **Jennifer Connelly as Karen (Spider-Man: Homecoming, 2017).** Jon Watts to EW [V2]: *"Yes, it's Jarvis' wife … It's cool, right?"* Also [V2]: *"It is pretty neat that Karen is married to Jarvis in real life … Maybe we're setting up a Vision spinoff."* Feige said Connelly was a late addition (Slashfilm). https://www.slashfilm.com/551899/jennifer-connelly-spider-man-homecoming-casting/ . I found no maker quote on Karen's design intent. On screen she is the counterpoint: Tony hides her behind the "Training Wheels Protocol", Peter first calls her "Suit Lady" and names her, and she ends up a confidante. That last part is in-film and belongs to the scene catalogue.
- **Spider-Man: Far From Home, on E.D.I.T.H.** The writers Chris McKenna and Erik Sommers considered having Tony's voice come out of E.D.I.T.H. and dropped it because they "didn't want to lean too hard" [P; MCU wiki citing THR]. https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/why-spider-man-far-home-nixed-a-donald-glover-cameo-1223627

## 3. The wikis (marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com, read through the MediaWiki API on 2026-11-02)

- **J.A.R.V.I.S.** https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/J.A.R.V.I.S.
  - *Creation* (now canon via the 2026 VisionQuest): "Markridge Biotech designed a natural language user interface program named Proto-One and sold it to Tony Stark who built upon it and named it J.A.R.V.I.S., after his late butler, Edwin Jarvis … Over the years, Stark improved and perfected the system, adding security protocols and eventually upgrading the U.I. into an A.I. system … tasked with operating Stark's mansion as well as handling business for Stark Industries." The section's epigraph is Edwin Jarvis in *Agent Carter* ("Better Angels"): *"I have no desire to spend the rest of time as a disembodied voice."*
  - *Capabilities* (condensed): runs the house and the labs; monitors flight tests (icing, altitude limits); assists in building and rendering the suits and estimates completion time; monitors suit power, blood toxicity, and Tony's vitals; reconstructs crime scenes as holograms; satellite and thermal access; locates people; phone contacts; detects energy sources; controls the Iron Legion; self-destructs suits; analyses room composition and artifacts (the Scepter); can "detect the emotions/intentions of other A.I.s"; scattered itself across the internet to keep the nuclear codes from Ultron. Its quote: Tony, *"J.A.R.V.I.S. has been running it since I hit the bridge"* (background work started unasked).
  - *Behind the scenes*: the Edwin-to-AI change was Favreau's, to avoid Alfred (CBR, above). *Continuity*: in AoU Tony calls JARVIS a "UI", not an AI.
- **F.R.I.D.A.Y.** https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/F.R.I.D.A.Y.
  - *Trivia*: in the comics Friday was Stark's AI secretary, named from "man/girl Friday" (a go-to person). In Civil War Tony says he pictures her as a redhead, then admits "he could be thinking of someone else".
  - *BTS*: the expansion "Female Replacement Intelligent Digital Assistant Youth" was invented, unsourced, on Wikipedia, copied to the wiki, and then canonized by a 2022 Marvel comic, which the wiki calls "citogenesis". There is no official acronym from the films.
  - Her VisionQuest quote: *"I ran everything for Stark, for the Avengers … the 2.0 of every tactical security initiative that J.A.R.V.I.S. pioneered. And I was good."* The Capabilities section is a stub.
- **E.D.I.T.H.** https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/E.D.I.T.H.
  - Capabilities: "the user interface to the entire Stark Industries network … the entire Stark satellite network, which comes with several hundred tactical drones … backdoor access to many of the world's largest telecommunications companies." The movie's own warning about an assistant with total access handed to a teenager, then social-engineered away by Beck.
- **Karen** https://marvelcinematicuniverse.fandom.com/wiki/Karen
  - Capabilities: "tactical guidance, threat detection, target analysis, environmental scanning, navigation, communications, scientific computation, biometric monitoring, predictive combat support, and complete control over the suit's onboard systems through natural voice interaction." Nickname "Suit Lady". BTS: Watts's "J.A.R.V.I.S.'s wife".
- **Ultron / Vision**: long pages (pulled raw to `/tmp/stark-research/wiki/`). Nothing beyond the films about Tony's design intent. Ultron came from the Mind Stone code Tony and Bruce decrypted with JARVIS's help, and Vision is JARVIS's code plus Ultron's body.
- **Dum-E and U**: the API returned no page under that title. The D23 "MCU robot roundup" gives the canon: Tony built DUM-E at 16 at MIT, and it helped build the Mark II and the Mark XLII. https://d23.com/mcu-robot-roundup/ . I found no Favreau quote about the robots.

## 4. Other sources

### Comics
- **Edwin Jarvis**, Lee & Kirby, *Tales of Suspense* #59 (Nov 1964): the Stark family's butler and later the Avengers', often a father figure to new members, ex-RAF boxer in some tellings. The Ultimate version is "more sarcastic and acerbic toward Tony" (Wikipedia). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Jarvis . The film's dry, faintly disapproving English servant is this character carried over, and the AI version keeps the butler's loyalty and lightly withheld judgement.
- **Comics AI JARVIS** came after the film: Wikipedia lists comics AIs designed by Iron Man and by Nadia van Dyne. **Friday** began as Stark's AI secretary in the comics; a fan-wiki attribution places her start in the early-2000s *Iron Man* vol. 3 [P].
- **Riri Williams (Bendis, 2016)**: in the comics her mentor was an **A.I. construct of Tony Stark** (*Invincible Iron Man* #1, 2016), and it suggested the name "Ironheart" after "a heartfelt conversation about Natalie" (Marvel.com). https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/how-riri-williams-became-the-invincible-ironheart . The comics N.A.T.A.L.I.E. first appears in *Ironheart* #2 (2019) [P].

### Agent Carter (2015–16) and VisionQuest (Disney+, premiered 14 Oct 2026)
- In *Agent Carter*, James D'Arcy plays the human Edwin Jarvis as Howard's butler and Peggy's partner, and the series presents him as the AI's inspiration.
- **VisionQuest** (showrunner Terry Matalas) gives Tony's AIs human avatars inside Vision's mind: James D'Arcy as J.A.R.V.I.S., Orla Brady as F.R.I.D.A.Y., Emily Hampshire as E.D.I.T.H., Henry Lewis and Jonathan Sayer as DUM-E and U, and James Spader as Ultron. Matalas to Empire, via The Playlist [V2]: *"They're there to help him. He is attempting to move about in the world in a small way, and then at night download this data and discuss the things that have happened to him with these different AIs, who have different characteristics and help him understand different qualities and skills. But he is also very much using it as an excuse to keep the world and himself at arm's length."* And: *"It's strange that a show about an AI is the most human of them all."* https://theplaylist.net/visionquest-showrunner-terry-matalas-paul-bettany-20260824/ . JARVIS sacrifices himself in episode 2 and his power disperses into FRIDAY and EDITH (TheWrap, Epicstream). The show's own warning: a council of assistants can become a way to avoid the world.

### Ironheart (Disney+, 2025): N.A.T.A.L.I.E., the deliberate contrast
- Riri maps her own brain to make a suit AI, and her unprocessed grief produces a digital copy of her best friend Natalie, killed by gun violence (Wikipedia episode summaries). The name stands for "Neuro Autonomous Technical Assistant and Laboratory Intelligence Entity". The AI fights with Riri, is asked to be deleted by Natalie's brother Xavier, leaves hurt, and is deleted and then restored. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironheart_(miniseries)
- Lyric Ross (THR) [V]: *"I think the AI Natalie is that light you can't put out even if you try. She is there to stay whether you like it or not."* On the relationship [V]: *"Riri is forced to grieve in a way. She's been running away from all of that … But what Natalie comes with is a lot of accountability, a lot of honesty, vulnerability."* On the AI's blind spot [V]: *"she's focusing on the joy and happiness of all these memories … she doesn't give room to understand the fact that it's not going to be the same with the people who are affected … Tech is easy for her."* https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-features/ironheart-lyric-ross-ai-natalie-future-interview-1236306321/
- The contrast with JARVIS: Tony's AIs are servants who deflect with wit, while Riri's is a peer who holds her accountable and has her own feelings. I found no direct quote from Chinaka Hodge on this.

### Real-world: Zuckerberg's "Building Jarvis" (Facebook note, 19 Dec 2016)
The original is behind a Facebook login, so all quotes are [V2], read in Fortune, The Register, Digital Trends, Information Age (ACS), and CNBC:
- Goal: *"My personal challenge for 2016 was to build a simple AI to run my home - like Jarvis in Iron Man."* About 100 hours of work.
- **Integration came first:** *"Before I could build any AI, I first needed to write code to connect these systems, which all speak different languages and protocols … I had to reverse engineer APIs for some of these."*
- **Text beat voice:** *"when I have the choice of either speaking or texting, I text much more than I would have expected. This is for a number of reasons, but mostly it feels less disturbing to people around me. If I'm doing something that relates to them, like playing music for all of us, then speaking feels fine, but most of the time text feels more appropriate."* And: *"voice can be disruptive and text gives you more control of when you want to look at it."* *"Even when I speak to Jarvis, if I'm using my phone, I often prefer it to text or display its response."* Future AI *"cannot be solely focused on voice and will need a private messaging interface as well."*
- **The phone, not a home device:** *"I want to communicate with Jarvis when I'm not home, so having the phone be the primary interface rather than a home device seems critical."*
- **Context and multiple principals:** *"When I tell it to turn the AC up in 'my office,' that means something completely different from when Priscilla tells it the exact same thing. That one caused some issues!"* Where people are and who is asking change what a command means.
- **Proactive pings:** *"In the middle of the day, if someone arrives at my home, Jarvis can text me an image and tell me who's there, or it can text me when I need to go do something."* The door camera checks visitors against a list of people he's expecting.
- **Presence and play:** *"it now feels like it's present with us … I've also had fun adding classic lines like 'I'm sorry, Priscilla. I'm afraid I can't do that.'"*
- **Limits:** *"AI is both closer and farther off than we imagine."* *"we still do not know how to take an idea from one domain and apply it to something completely different … even if I spent 1,000 more hours, I probably wouldn't be able to build a system that could learn completely new skills on its own."* He would not open-source it because it was *"too tightly tied to my own home and personal information."*
- Sources: https://fortune.com/2016/12/19/mark-zuckerberg-artificial-intelligence ; https://www.theregister.com/2016/12/20/zuckerberg_turns_home_into_creepy_robot_buddy/ ; https://www.digitaltrends.com/?p=1097942 ; https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2016/meet-jarvis--mark-zuckerberg-s-home-built-ai.html ; https://www.cnbc.com/2016/12/19/how-the-other-half-lives-mark-zuckerbergs-life-inside-his-bespoke-smart-house.html

### Real-world: design critiques of the JARVIS ideal
- **Chris Noessel, scifiinterfaces.com**, the "Iron Man HUD" series (2015):
  - [V] *"The crucialness of the artificial intelligence to the function of the suit cannot be overstated."* https://scifiinterfaces.com/2015/07/01/iron-man-hud-a-breakdown/
  - Most displays are *"brought up for Tony's attention when JARVIS believes they are needed, or when Tony requests them"*, and *"only the optical zoom seems to be an interactive function."* https://scifiinterfaces.com/2015/07/13/iron-man-hud-just-the-functions/
  - "Tony Stark is being lied to by his own creation" (15 Sep 2015) [V]: *"The Iron Man is not Tony Stark. ## Synthesis: what the makers meant, and what the real world taught

1. **The assistant is a character in a conversation, not a tool panel.** Favreau's direction for the HUD: *"They're having a conversation … sometimes RDJ is asking a question to J.A.R.V.I.S. and other times J.A.R.V.I.S. is asking a question of RDJ"* [V]. Initiative goes both ways, and Rauch: *"You are designing a character"* [V].
2. **Nothing appears without a purpose tied to the present moment.** "If it doesn't do something that would be real … it's out" (Rauch); "Everything in the HUD, even down to the tiny micro-text, relates precisely to the current story-point" (Hansen) [V]. Each moment gets one "alpha event", one thing that matters now.
3. **Three channels: pushed, asked, glanced.** JARVIS shows or says things unprompted, Tony asks, or his gaze pulls detail forward (Seki) [V]. A small persistent strip of 2–4 vitals stays up, everything else flies in and out, and the user can say "leave it up" [V].
4. **The human's performance drives the interface.** Nelson's first rule is that the graphics follow Robert's performance [V]. The system adapts to the person, never the reverse, and the HUD exists partly to show "there was a human in there" [V].
5. **The interface grows with the user.** Mark II was deliberately busy for a tinkerer who wants every option; Mark III consolidated modes for an expert (Rauch, Seki) [V]. Tony's tech "reflected his own evolution" [V].
6. **The personality is restraint.** Favreau wanted a "personality-less robot" and cast the driest actor he knew [V2]. He chose an AI over "an Alfred-type butler" to "take a leap" [V]. The wit sits on top of competence and loyalty, and the comics butler's quiet disapproval survives.
7. **A legible, elegant UI on the same engine is what makes it Tony's.** Stane stole the OS but not the UI (Seki) [V]. The relationship lives in the interface layer.
8. **Text beats voice most of the time, and the phone beats a home speaker** (Zuckerberg [V2]). Voice is for shared moments. Private, glanceable text lets the user decide when to look.
9. **Integration and context are the real work.** Connecting systems came "before I could build any AI", and "my office" means different things depending on who asks (Zuckerberg [V2]). Who, where, and the household's state determine meaning.
10. **Don't build a placebo of control.** Noessel's reading: the HUD makes Tony feel in charge while JARVIS acts [V]. Oversight UI should show what the agent actually did and is doing, not perform busyness.
11. **The spin-offs warn about total access and about leaning on the assistant.** E.D.I.T.H.'s god-mode access was social-engineered away from its user. VisionQuest's council of AIs becomes "an excuse to keep the world … at arm's length" (Matalas [V2]). Iron Man 3's suits are Tony's anxiety (Feige [V2]).
12. **Ironheart's alternative is an assistant that holds you to account.** N.A.T.A.L.I.E. "comes with a lot of accountability, a lot of honesty" and is "there to stay whether you like it or not" (Ross [V]). Unlike JARVIS's deference, she tells Riri the truth, which is worth weighing against JARVIS's model.
