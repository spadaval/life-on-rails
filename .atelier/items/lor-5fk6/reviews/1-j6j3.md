## Finding 1: Open searched tasks independently of membership in the queue
Recommendation: fix now

`android/homecore/src/webMain/kotlin/com/spadaval/lor/concepts/queue/Drawer.kt:235` offers every matched task and sets `Sheet.Item("task:<id>")`, but `Sheets.kt:65-68` immediately closes any non-thread item absent from `store.items()`. That list contains only flagged and due tasks. `Detail.kt:100` also resolves tasks only through that queue. From `#queue/demo`, search for `walnut` and tap “Cut the walnut boards to length”: the undated task appears in search, but its detail immediately disappears. `/tmp/lor-5fk6-review/search-undated-task.png` and its anchors capture the result. Future-dated tasks fail for the same reason, and a flagged task is keyed `flag:<id>` in the queue rather than search's `task:<id>`.

Resolve searched tasks against the full task store, retain their detail while the task exists, and route them to an actually visible detail surface on both screen sizes. This preserves the brief's common search capability without putting undated/future tasks into the ranked queue.

## Finding 2: Use one advancing clock for timer writes and every readout
Recommendation: fix now

`android/homecore/src/webMain/kotlin/com/spadaval/lor/concepts/kit/Store.kt:219-232` starts and switches a timer using `now`, an immutable instant parsed from the fixture at store creation. Meanwhile `queue/Queue.kt:198-205` starts a separate elapsed clock each time a `liveNow` call enters composition. Starting a timer after spending a minute in the viewer therefore backdates it by that minute on home/queue; opening its sheet starts that sheet's clock again at the fixture instant, making its readout jump backwards. Switching a running timer likewise retains time elapsed since opening the state rather than starting the new label at zero. I reproduced this with a timer started eight seconds after opening the state: the sheet showed `0:01`, and closing it immediately returned to a queue readout of `0:22` (`/tmp/lor-5fk6-review/timer-sheet-confirmed.png` and `timer-queue-confirmed.png`). This makes the same timer disagree between its surfaces during ordinary use, despite the “one timer” requirement.

Keep one elapsed-time origin in the store, use its current simulated instant for Start/Switch, and have all readouts observe that same clock. The fixture can remain the base instant; no real service connection is needed.
